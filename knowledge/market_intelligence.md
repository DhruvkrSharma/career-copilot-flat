### Key Shift: Specialization over Generalization

The generic "ML Engineer" title is fragmenting into specialized roles:
- **Applied ML Engineer** — end-to-end model development for products
- **MLOps / LLMOps Engineer** — infrastructure, serving, monitoring
- **AI Infrastructure Engineer** — GPU clusters, distributed training, cost
- **AI Product Engineer** — LLM integration, AI UX, prompt management
- **AI Safety Engineer** — guardrails, red teaming, compliance

## What Recruiters Actually Look For (2026)

### Top 6 Signals (in priority order)
1. **Production-deployed projects** — Not notebooks. Live demos, APIs, containerized apps, or agentic systems with real users.
2. **Agentic AI experience** — Built multi-agent systems, tool-calling workflows, or autonomous task-completing agents. This is the #1 differentiator in 2026.
3. **Clean GitHub with READMEs** — Pinned repos, documentation, architecture diagrams, CI/CD badges.
4. **End-to-end ownership** — "I designed, built, deployed, and monitored this system" — not "my team worked on it."
5. **System design fluency** — Can discuss trade-offs (latency vs cost vs accuracy), not just implement tutorials.
6. **Evaluation mindset** — Can explain how they measured success beyond "accuracy went up." Knows about LLM evaluation, drift detection, A/B testing.

### Emerging Requirements (2026-specific)
- **Agentic AI depth** — Not just calling APIs. Building planning loops, tool registries, error recovery, and multi-agent coordination.
- **MCP / A2A protocol knowledge** — Emerging standard for agent interoperability. Early adopters stand out.
- **AI cost awareness** — Can discuss cost-per-query, token optimization, model routing, and when to use smaller vs larger models.
- **Multi-modal experience** — Projects combining vision + language + audio (Gemini, GPT-5.5 Vision, Claude Vision).
- **Responsible AI knowledge** — Guardrails, prompt injection defense, bias auditing, red teaming.
- **Production observability** — LangSmith, LangFuse, Helicone — monitoring AI systems in production.

## Key Market Themes (2026)

1. **Production > Prototypes** — Companies moved past the "AI experiment" phase. They want engineers who ship reliable, monitored, cost-efficient AI systems.
2. **Agents are the new apps** — Building autonomous agents that plan, reason, use tools, and complete multi-step tasks is the hottest skill.
3. **Evaluation is eating the world** — "How do you know it works?" is the most important question. LLM-as-judge, golden trajectories, and automated eval pipelines are expected knowledge.
4. **Cost consciousness** — GPT-5.5 is expensive. Companies want engineers who can route between models, cache intelligently, and optimize token usage.
5. **Skills > Degrees** — Portfolio and production experience outweigh credentials. A strong GitHub profile beats a PhD from a mediocre program.
